How to read the answer

The tool scores your draft out of 10 across four dimensions (Clarity, Structure, Engagement, and Goal Alignment), averages them with the lowest score so a weak area cannot hide, and caps the overall label at NOT PROVED YET whenever your text makes an unproved claim or lacks figures. It prints Your Text, With the Findings Marked (showing exact buzzwords, Flesch readability score out of 100, missing questions, low "you" count, missing figures, unproved superlatives, and missing call to action), followed by Edits Made and an Improved Version. The Improved Version swaps flagged buzzwords for plain words and adds structure, but you still must replace unproved claims with a real measurement and add your own call to action.

What to do next

  • Founder: Replace every [unproved: ...] and [buzzword: ...] span marked in Your Text, With the Findings Marked with a concrete customer figure and plain buyer words.
  • CEO: Check Purpose confirmation and Goal Alignment to ensure the draft states one clear buyer outcome and asks for one specific next step.
  • CMO: Use the Quick Checklist and What This Audience Looks For to set an editorial gate so no page or email ships with unproved superlatives or a NOT PROVED YET label.
  • CRO: Run outbound sales emails and proposal summaries through the tool to strip vendor jargon and add a clear buyer question and call to action.
  • Demand generation head: Edit your landing page or campaign copy using the Improved Version, add a verified customer metric and a clear call to action, and re-score it before launch.

Why come back

Run the improver again immediately after editing your draft to confirm the buzzwords are gone, the Flesch readability score has improved, and the NOT PROVED YET flag is cleared. Come back whenever you draft a new homepage hero, cold email, LinkedIn post, or sales deck slide. It runs the same fixed rules every time in your browser or in your own assistant, and your inputs are not stored or sent to an AI model.

Who is the CRAFT Content Improver tool not ideal for?

  • Anyone who cannot give the inputs listed above, because the answer is built only from them.
  • Teams that want finished copy in their brand voice: each tool returns a structured draft to edit, with placeholders wherever your inputs did not supply a fact.
  • Anyone who needs research or quotes gathered for them: the tools do not look anything up. They work from the story and facts you give, and anything they add themselves is marked as an example, a suggestion or a placeholder.
